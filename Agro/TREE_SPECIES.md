## Botanical basis

Dimensions below describe ordinary mature plants in suitable conditions. They
are calibration references, not clipping planes or promises for a given soil,
light environment or establishment age.

| Preset | Mature reference | Leaves and habit | Flowers and fruit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Celtis australis | Usually 15–20 m tall, about 10–12 m broad | Broad, eventually domed tree; alternate serrated asymmetric blades, 5–15 cm long in the European Atlas (4–10 cm in NSW Flora); yellow autumn foliage | Small green spring flowers; roughly 10 mm dark drupes in late summer/autumn |
| Amelanchier alnifolia 'Obelisk' | Common nursery description 3–5 m tall, 1–2 m broad; some horticultural sources give a larger ultimate height category | Dense ascending column, commonly several stems; alternate rounded elliptic blades with teeth mainly towards the tip; bronze young leaves and yellow/orange autumn foliage | White mid-spring flowers in short racemes, then purple-black pomes about 10–15 mm across |
| Acer platanoides 'Globosum' | Usually 5–7 m high and broad, dependent on graft height | Dense rounded crown, flattening with age, on a trained standard; opposite palmately lobed blades and yellow autumn foliage | Yellow-green spring corymbs; paired samaras, each with a nutlet and a wing |

## Establishment and architecture

The Plants tab's signed age applies to every species. Zero means an ungerminated
seed at recording start; positive values introduce a seed later; negative values
introduce a germinated propagule earlier and simulate its actual shared history.
See [PLANT_ESTABLISHMENT.md](PLANT_ESTABLISHMENT.md) for the clock, rounding,
checkpoint, weather and measurement contracts. A nominal year is 365 days.

The woody establishment helper transfers the supplied reserve and water between
an initial root, an anchored root collar, and small shoots. It does not construct
an adult or nursery-sized snapshot. The internal root-collar `IsRizome` flag is a
mechanical anchor; it does not imply that these species spread by rhizomes.

Obelisk begins with three basal stems. Celtis begins with one. The maple's
rootstock grows to its editable 2.2 m graft height, then its apex is replaced by
four spreading framework shoots using mature scion material. Low permanent
branches are suppressed below the editable clear-trunk height. Establishment
leaves remain until an actual crown exists, avoiding starvation of a young bare
rootstock. Cultivar identity is fixed by the selected preset: the generic seed
stage does not simulate genetic segregation or reproduce the real nursery
propagation process.

Persistent wood and buds survive leaf fall. Long shoots produce new nodes;
compressed short shoots renew individually simulated petioles and leaves each
spring. Short-shoot support expands before the foliage is admitted. Alternate
and opposite arrangements follow `LateralsPerNode` and `LateralRoll`; daughter
axes on the existing path have distinct axial phases. Celtis' enabled
dorsiventral units instead carry their two ranks in the branch frame and
initialize each new lateral plane using that axis's own heading.

A dormant bud receives at most one long-shoot trial per growing season. A failed
trial is not a permanent prohibition: its subsequent probability declines with
bud age and depends on exposure and available resources. Long-shoot extension
declines with plant age, branch order and crown reach. Height and radius enter
smooth attenuation functions; the simulator does not clip, delete or shorten
wood at the reference mature dimensions. Radial growth is independent of axial
extension and uses the same seasonal rate when contact suspends a shoot.
The annual extension counter follows final accepted geometry after construction
funding and contact shortening, including across new same-axis continuation
nodes. Trial contact poses do not change the counter. Initial
basal foliage waits until its actual attachment clears the soil by 5 mm.
Reserve-funded emergence remains available until that first clearance, including
for slower multistem seedlings whose normal spring opening interval has ended.
This exposure bridge is restricted to a leafless primary axis and still obeys
funding, hydration, temperature, season and annual-extension limits.

The annual axillary long-shoot trial waits until renewed foliage has completed two daily
resource blocks and a blade has expanded to at least80% of both target length and
width. Tiny spring leaves do not yet cast the shade of the developed crown;
existing terminal shoots still flush from reserves. Newly born leaves temporarily inherit the formation's resource
maximum for allocation; using that placeholder as measured local irradiance
would activate shaded buds as though they were in full sun. The waiting check
visits only the short shoot's local leaf units and adds no global scan.

Since checkpoint50, woody laminae carry their own previous-step shortwave
radiosity by stable organ identity. The former per-plant mean could distribute
light between bright and dark crown regions without a local optical path.
Removal and reindexing preserve the surviving lamina's value; new laminae start
from the common analytic estimate. The sensor sums both faces, so the lumped
emission divides reflected energy between two hemispheres. Existing species and
non-lamina woody organs retain their previous bulk optical approximation.
This does not resolve separate spectral reflection/transmission or exact
front/back leaf-surface scattering.

Short shoots also accumulate active-season suppression under sustained local
shade. The default tree profile uses180 active-season days, below both5% of the
daily vegetative light reference and15% of the crown's daily mean leaf exposure.
These are provisional model coefficients, not measured cultivar constants.
Bright periods reduce the accumulated hours; winter preserves them. A suppressed
spur retires at its next spring renewal, preserving the current year's foliage
and fruit support. Uniform whole-tree shade alone does not meet the relative
threshold. Set `TreeShortShootShadeDiebackDays` to zero to disable the response.
The counter is physiological state: deferred development retains it, and live
checkpoints persist it. The permanent collar's already-computed daily leaf mean
supplies the crown reference; no additional whole-tree walk is needed.

The mechanism is motivated by branch autonomy and asymmetric competition, as
demonstrated for mountain birch by
[differential branch versus whole-tree shading](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28547232/).
That experiment does not establish these three species' thresholds or timing.
Norway maple's shade-tolerant whole-plant survival, described by
[Martin et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2745.2010.01666.x),
must not be confused with a fixed mortality rate for its individual short shoots.

Since checkpoint48, Obelisk also forms axillary short-shoot daughters. After a
failed long-shoot trial, an exposed, funded spur can form one woody daughter at
its distal existing leaf axil. This daughter retains the same long-shoot order,
starts as a real bud and first renews foliage the following spring. Its support,
resource transfers, contacts and annual decision use the ordinary developmental
transactions. A spur with a woody daughter cannot add another; the resulting
short-shoot chain does not fork exponentially. The provisional annual rate is
0.20 before light reduction. The control defaults to zero for other species and
does not consume additional random draws when disabled.

On the inherited-inclination path, each long shoot retains its gravity-relative inclination, sampled when its basal,
lateral or grafted axis is created. Continuation internodes inherit that angle.
Directional-light guidance blends the light's inclination with this architectural
setpoint, using `TreePhototropicWeight` (default 0.25), and keeps responding to
light azimuth. An effectively vertical sky preserves the shoot's own azimuth.
The existing extension-dependent, timestep-scaled turning rate still applies.
This balances light sensing with branch architecture rather than progressively
turning every internode upright. The balance is a model choice supported in
principle by [Duchemin et al. (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00964); neither that
paper nor the botanical sources establish 0.25 as a measured cultivar constant.
Enabled finite growth units instead retain a unit reference and accepted-length
light response/path across internodes and contact trials. Full three-dimensional
cue strength and the accepted unit budget limit response; a tiny horizontal
projection cannot introduce a full azimuth turn. Constant-light mode0 supplies
no measured directional cue. These mechanisms do not themselves validate an
adult crown under CPU light.

The inherited angle entered live checkpoint version 41; version 42 added the
corrected soil-line reference described below. Version 46 also
requires the corrected woody root growth law, capacity-relative branching signal
and conserved uptake budget. It replaces version 43's storage-recharge boolean
with the next tick's bounded water request. Older live
checkpoints are rejected; their recorded playback remains readable. Version47
additionally stored the actual root-segment birth role described below.
Version50 added local lamina radiosity and cumulative short-shoot suppression.
Those features remain in the maintained checkpoint58 implementation. Versions49
and50 below refer to historical isolated profiles; their layouts are not the
current writer. Checkpoint58 additionally binds effective module controls and
preserves accepted module/light/secondary-material and samara-unit state; V9
playback and older saved-scene readers remain unchanged.

`TreeMainlineModuleMode` is numeric Off0/RealAxillary1/Auto2; class, missing-value
and preset defaults remain Off. Explicit RealAxillary validates a supported
single-stem ungrafted form and uses its declared training endpoint. Auto resolves
once from the requested form: supported forms use the clear-trunk target as the
effective training endpoint, while unsupported forms keep the existing model.
Requested stem count, graft and raw training controls remain editable and are
not overwritten. New real-axillary modules keep distinct identity, actual
petiole/site ancestry and accepted material clocks; they are not inferred merely
from functional branch order. Their opening/arch programme reuses the configured
lateral arch, not a separately measured Celtis trunk-development law.

Secondary erection is separately opt-in through positive maturation strain.
The current trial requires an actual Live successor, a cycle after predecessor
closure and positive accepted radius growth. It acts over a configured fraction
of actual formed module length; the opening fraction instead refers to frozen
allowance, so equal fractions do not imply equal physical zones. Ring-associated
rest-curvature changes and ancestor transport are distinct from passive loaded
deflection. The signed inclination/curvature drive and the trial timing/zone
are provisional; instantaneous-ring mechanics does not supply a species-specific
maturation lag or a closed carbon/work account. No strain/zone calibration or
mature default is established by the focused rollback tests.

Graft height, clear trunk height and the vertical crown envelope are measured
from the soil line, independently of the seed's burial depth. Each plant caches
the first upward soil-surface intersection from its planting column, using the
existing static hierarchy. The query ignores obstacles and chooses the nearest
crossing in stacked soil layers; the collider does not expose per-soil identity.
When the planting point is outside soil or no upward surface is found, the
planting origin is the explicit fallback. This derived cache is reconstructed
from the unchanged static geometry on resume. Roots and the planting position
are not moved. A leaning stock reaches its graft by actual vertical projection;
horizontal or downward growth cannot trigger grafting merely by adding length.

The architecture remains a developmental model, not a measured reconstruction
of a particular specimen. Compressed-node renewal does not resolve every annual
growth scar, individual bud scale or anatomical graft union.

Bud break, growth cessation and leaf fall use the existing March-to-February

carbon price for new tissue. Setting a positive cost enables the construction
budget, including buds, branches, fruit, wings and aggregate roots. Maintenance,

unit system. These presets are not a validated predictive carbon or biomass
budget; conserving transferred water/reserve pools does not establish that claim.

## Flowers, fruit and shared geometry

Reproduction is attached to eligible exposed buds/renewed short shoots. A thick
parent that encloses a dormant spur does not acquire leaves or flowers through
its bark. Flower buds must expand before anthesis; blocked or unfunded buds do
not flower simply because a calendar duration elapsed. Inflorescence axes and
pedicels start small, grow through the shared floral support helper, and must
reach their targets before anthesis. Temperature, exposure, resources, water and
contacts constrain that growth. Their initial spacing allows distinct embryonic
buds to be admitted without coincident geometry.

Obelisk's flowers are distributed along a raceme. Maple corymbs have staggered
axial attachments and longer lower pedicels so the flower heads approach a common
plane. Pedicel attachment offsets follow the actual axis as it expands. Celtis
fruit-bearing flowers attach by individual 25 mm target pedicels, without a
shared corymb axis. A deterministic third of flowering sites instead carries a
small male group; that share is a model coefficient, not a measured sex ratio.
Those flowers are sterile in the model and cannot set fruit. The five green
Celtis perianth parts use the shared lamina-organ representation; they represent
sepals, not a claim that this apetalous species has showy petals.

Fruit set is sampled once per flower, including contact retries. The maple
creates **two separate nutlet bodies, each with one wing**. The body convention
is full longitudinal `Length` and transverse half-diameter `Radius`; body volume
is `2π/3 × Length × Radius²`. A wing uses the same thin-lamina convention as a
leaf. Construction accounting follows those shapes when positive tissue cost is
enabled. Petals, fruits and wings cast shadows but do not photosynthesize or
contribute leaf area to the leaf-area-index sensors.

Expansion depends on temperature, water, exposure and available reserves.
Ripening begins after actual mature dimensions are reached; support stalks stay
until their children are shed. Retention is configured separately from ripening.
There is no dispersal, animal feeding, explicit pollen transport or genetic
offspring model. Each preset's fruit-set probability is a calibrated proxy.

Contact-retained births retain their sampled organs and random decisions.
Admission rebases newly created reproductive phase clocks, including a valid
zero timestamp. Spring flower commitments and admitted unopened buds expire
outside their flowering window plus the configured development allowance; a
drought-delayed spring bud cannot produce its first petals in late summer.
Checkpoint state includes these phases, pending transactions and fruit decisions.

Leaf outlines, flower perianths, fruit bodies and samara wings use shared
geometry for contacts, CPU shadows and exports. Celtis has an asymmetric serrated
blade, Obelisk a rounded blade with distal teeth, and maple a deeply lobed blade.
The browser's WebGL, WebGPU and GLTF paths consume the same body/wing assets.
Requested Mitsuba/Tamashii lighting remains external while the active geometry
fits their protocol. Live deformable foliage or unsupported reproductive organs
use the CPU solver; pending, empty and dead formations do not trigger that
fallback. Removing the unsupported geometry restores external lighting and its
facade measurements, while an HTTP failure retains the existing offline CPU
policy. Explicit constant-light and CPU modes remain unchanged.
Woody attachment offsets are inherited once by descendants and transported with
ancestor rotations through gravity, contacts, pending births and rollback. A
short-shoot petiole therefore carries its leaf, a shifted pedicel carries its
flower, and each offset maple nutlet carries its own wing.

## Roots and scale

Explicit root agents remain the default. Woody roots admit new segments and
extension only while their bases/tips remain in the selected soil. This is
independent of the `Taproot` architecture setting. `SimulationRequest.VirtualRoots=true`
selects an aggregate occupied-volume proxy, now usable for establishment,
uptake, maintenance, reserve distribution and checkpoints. Its soil sampling
uses at most 27 cells per plant per tick. The proxy conserves allocated water
and energy, pays positive construction cost on occupied tissue, never reports
a negative available energy pool and dies after sustained starvation.

Woody root extension uses the editable `TreeRootAnnualExtension` (initial model
calibration: 0.65 m per nominal 365-day year). Secondary root growth uses
`TreeAnnualRadiusGrowth` over the same year. Both rates are multiplied by bounded
daily resource availability, current tissue hydration and soil-temperature
response. This replaces the legacy inverse-volume multipliers, which at a
newborn root's dimensions allowed about 7.6 mm/h axial and 199 mm/h radial growth
before geometric clamps. Younger distal segments consequently have less time to
thicken. `RootRadiusTaper` applies at a lateral axis's origin. Serial continuations,
including continuations of a lateral axis, inherit the full parent radius ceiling.
The persisted `StartsLateralAxis` bit is set by the actual birth operation; it is
not inferred from sibling count, orientation or age. Adding a lateral must not
repeatedly narrow the pre-existing continuing axis. Existing radii never shrink
when a ceiling changes. This is a developmental rate model,
not a measured universal taper curve or an imposed root-depth limit.

Version 46 still applied the factor to every daughter at a junction. In its
11.4-year Celtis diagnostic, an inferred continuing path accumulated 34 junctions
over 1.15 m, leaving older distal segments near a 0.975 mm ceiling. Version 47
removes this discretization artifact without changing growth rates or imposing
a count or mass cap. Its lateral ratio remains provisional; a full-width
continuation and a thinner lateral do not constitute an area-conserving pipe
model. Architectural models likewise distinguish parent-axis and lateral-root
diameters ([Pagès, 2012](https://doi.org/10.2136/vzj2011.0152)); this source does
not establish the preset's numerical ratio.

Woody newborn resource shares are limited by each child's actual water storage;
unused water returns to its donor before immediate or staged admission. Once
contact geometry is final, displaced tissue water moves to the existing conserved
plant reserve. An admitted flower-to-fruit owner is settled after commitment,
since that conversion can reduce its capacity. Trial poses and pending proposals
cannot credit the reserve. This avoids false support loads from water forced
into microscopic leaves, fruit or wings. The settlement shares the existing
linear construction pass and leaves herbaceous behavior unchanged.

The axial coefficient is provisional. A thirteen-provenance
[Celtis nursery study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10457-004-2948-x) reported one-year
root and shoot growth of 30.5 ± 5.4 and 61.1 ± 13.3 cm, with roots contributing
24.6% of biomass. Those measurements provide context, not an exact target for
this soil, climate or simulated total root length. The default resource factor
reduces the maximum rate. Nursery allocation, timestep dependence and mature
supply require measured acceptance before this coefficient is treated as stable.

No new woody lateral branch trial runs at zero biological water demand; a
previously retained trial waits without being discarded or redrawn. Existing
axes can still extend and thicken under favourable soil conditions while the
crown is leafless. Soil recharge remains controlled by aggregate tissue-storage
shortfall, including conserved unallocated water; a rounding residue cannot
block uptake. No water is deleted and no root-count cap is imposed.

For woody uptake, a root can conduct water through its tissue even while its own
storage is full. Its local proposal is limited by absorptive surface, soil
temperature, vacant local storage and the existing modeled onward flow. Census
caches one whole-plant budget: the smaller of (a) total anatomical capacity plus
the photosynthetic forecast minus all existing plant water, including unallocated
reserve, and (b) vacant root storage plus flow through the **basal roots only**.
Both terms are nonnegative. Counting only basal flow prevents a serial axis's
subdivision from multiplying its conducting capacity. The request router groups
proposals by soil cell, limits each group to available water, then scales all
eligible groups proportionally to that budget. Dry proximal cells therefore do
not consume the allowance needed by wet distal cells, and root iteration order
does not greedily allocate the plant budget. Existing soil grants still arbitrate
competition between plants.

The aggregate proxy uses the same conserved budget and temperature gate, with
flow estimated from its occupied cross-section. This is a bounded transport
approximation, not a water-potential or pressure-network solver. The inherited
1.8 m/h flow-velocity coefficient is a model assumption, not a measured hydraulic
conductivity for these species. Legacy explicit roots retain their prior local
storage limit. No additional water reserve is introduced by the correction.

For woody lateral initiation, each Census computes the allocation signal
`max(0, 1 - rootWaterCapacity / shootWaterCapacity)` from its existing gathered
capacities. With no shoot capacity the signal is zero. Its finite reciprocal is
stored in the existing `WaterBalanceUG` field, so roots read one cached value
and checkpoint continuation preserves it. Both tissues use a 0.75 water-volume
fraction; this compares occupied tissue volume rather than current hydration.
It avoids treating a large water reserve inside wood as proof that the root
network is adequate. The equal-volume reference is a provisional allocation
choice, not an asserted optimal biomass ratio. It scales new lateral trials;
it does not shrink roots or cap existing-axis extension or thickening. Legacy
species retain their previous water-balance branch rule, and the aggregate
root proxy has no individual lateral-root trials.

Virtual roots do not reconstruct actual fine-root geometry. The above-ground
model still simulates individual organs. Mature small-leaved trees can require
many thousands of leaves and substantially more organs; multidecade preparation
is therefore real simulation work and may be expensive. The architecture adds
no all-pairs scan: each developmental update works on its organ and local
children, while the existing contact and light engines retain their own costs.
The preceding root versions failed whole-plant acceptance despite passing
focused conservation checks. Version 42 concentrated effective storage near the
collar through repeated serial taper and later exhausted maple water despite
wet distal cells. Version 43 corrected that taper and dormant recharge, but its
unbounded legacy root growth then produced 274,787 segments and 63.7 kg of roots
against 0.467 kg of shoot at 2.12 years. Suppressing only zero-demand lateral
trials prevented the count explosion but left a single 4.33 m axis uniformly
21.56 mm in radius at three years. These failed histories are preserved and are
not current-model acceptance. Version 45's fresh nursery histories removed the
root proliferation, but exposed a separate hydraulic failure: maple leaf water
storage in the first summer fell to 4.11% at 1 h ticks and 2.68% at 4 h ticks
despite wet soil. Those percentages concern the leaves themselves, not dry wood
diluting a whole-plant water/mass ratio. Both histories recharged in winter, and
the four-hour fifth-summer leaves recovered to 93.63%, but this later recovery
does not validate the first-season supply mechanism. The storage-only uptake
